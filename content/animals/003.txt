# Animals — batch 3: birds & insects
Owls are farsighted and can't see things very close to their eyes clearly.
Owls fly almost silently thanks to special fringed feathers.
Crows can use tools, like bending wires into hooks to fish out food.
New Caledonian crows can solve multi-step puzzles.
Ravens can plan for the future, in experiments, by saving tools they'll need later.
Magpies can recognise themselves in a mirror.
Parrots can learn to copy human speech because of their specially shaped brains and voice boxes.
Some parrots name their chicks with unique calls.
The kea, a New Zealand parrot, is famous for being mischievous and tearing apart car parts.
Pigeons can recognise human faces and find their way home over long distances.
Pigeons were used to carry messages in both World Wars.
The Arctic tern makes the longest migration of any animal, flying from the Arctic to Antarctica and back each year.
Over its lifetime, an Arctic tern may fly a distance equal to several trips to the Moon and back.
The bar-tailed godwit can fly over 11,000 km nonstop across the Pacific.
Swifts can stay in the air for months without landing, even sleeping on the wing.
Ostriches lay the biggest eggs of any living bird.
Ostriches can run at about 70 km/h.
Ostriches don't bury their heads in the sand. That's a myth.
Kiwi birds lay eggs that are huge compared with their body size.
Penguins can't fly, but they "fly" underwater using their wings as flippers.
Flamingos often stand on one leg, possibly to save energy.
Woodpeckers can peck about 20 times per second.
Woodpeckers have special skull features that help protect their brains when pecking.
Hummingbirds can't walk or hop well. Their feet are mainly for perching.
Hummingbirds visit hundreds of flowers a day.
The bee hummingbird is the smallest bird in the world, about the size of a bee.
Eagles can spot prey from more than a kilometre away.
A bald eagle's nest can weigh over a tonne.
Vultures have strong stomach acid that helps them eat rotting meat safely.
Peacocks' famous tails are actually long feathers called a train.
The peacock's train can have around 200 feathers.
Male bowerbirds build and decorate elaborate structures to attract mates.
Lyrebirds can mimic almost any sound, including camera shutters and chainsaws.
The mynah bird is an excellent mimic too.
Hornbills seal the female inside a tree hole with mud while she nests. The male feeds her through a slit.
The Great Indian Bustard is one of the heaviest flying birds, and critically endangered.
India is home to more than 1,300 species of birds.
Bees are found on every continent except Antarctica.
A honeybee colony can have tens of thousands of bees.
Honeybees beat their wings about 200 times a second, which creates their buzz.
A queen bee can lay up to around 2,000 eggs a day.
Honey never truly spoils if it's stored properly.
Bees visit millions of flowers to make one kilogram of honey.
Bumblebees can fly at high altitudes, even above Mount Everest's height in lab experiments.
Ants have been farming fungus for millions of years.
Some ants farm aphids, protecting them in exchange for sweet honeydew.
Leafcutter ants can strip a tree of its leaves in a single night.
Army ants build living bridges and shelters out of their own bodies.
Fire ants can link together to form rafts that float during floods.
Termite queens can live for decades.
Dragonflies are among the most successful hunters in the animal world, catching most of the prey they chase.
Dragonflies have been around for over 300 million years. Ancient ones had wingspans of about 70 cm.
Butterflies' wings are covered in tiny scales.
Monarch butterflies migrate thousands of kilometres between Canada and Mexico.
No single monarch butterfly makes the full round trip. It takes several generations.
Fireflies glow through a chemical reaction that produces almost no heat.
Some fireflies flash in sync with each other.
Crickets "sing" by rubbing their wings together.
You can estimate the temperature by counting cricket chirps.
Cockroaches can survive for a while without their heads.
Cockroaches have been around since before the dinosaurs.
A housefly's wings beat about 200 times a second.
Mosquitoes are attracted to the carbon dioxide we breathe out.
Only female mosquitoes bite. They need blood to make eggs.
Mosquitoes are among the deadliest animals to humans because of the diseases they spread.
Spiders aren't insects. They're arachnids, with eight legs.
Spider silk is incredibly strong for its weight.
Jumping spiders have excellent eyesight and can see in colour.
Some spiders use their silk to fly, drifting on the wind. It's called ballooning.
The goliath birdeater is one of the largest spiders in the world, about the size of a dinner plate.
Dung beetles can navigate using the Milky Way.
Dung beetles can move dung balls many times their own weight.
Ladybirds can eat thousands of aphids in their lifetime.
Praying mantises can turn their heads 180 degrees.
Grasshoppers have ears on their bellies.
Some caterpillars disguise themselves as bird droppings.
A caterpillar basically dissolves inside its chrysalis before becoming a butterfly.
Silkworms produce silk from a single continuous thread that can be hundreds of metres long.
India is one of the world's largest producers of silk.
Insects make up the majority of known animal species on Earth.
