# Animals — batch 7: top-up
The Indian giant squirrel has bright maroon, orange and cream fur.
The lion-tailed macaque lives only in the Western Ghats.
The Nilgiri tahr is a mountain goat found only in southern India.
The hoolock gibbon is India's only ape.
The Gangetic river dolphin is India's national aquatic animal.
Gangetic dolphins are nearly blind and find food with echolocation.
The Indian rhinoceros has a single horn.
The Great Indian hornbill can live for about 50 years.
The Indian peafowl's call is one of the loudest bird calls in Indian forests.
The red panda is the state animal of Sikkim.
The Asiatic elephant has smaller ears than the African elephant.
Only some male Asian elephants have tusks.
The Indian wild ass lives in the Little Rann of Kutch.
The Himalayan monal, a colourful pheasant, is the state bird of Uttarakhand.
The dhole, or Indian wild dog, hunts in packs and whistles to communicate.
The sloth bear got its name because early naturalists thought it was related to sloths.
India has more than 400 species of reptiles.
The Malabar giant squirrel can leap up to about 6 metres between trees.
The fishing cat swims and dives to catch fish.
The fishing cat is the state animal of West Bengal.
India's Kaziranga has one of the highest densities of tigers in the world.
The Sundarbans tigers are known for swimming between islands.
Wild Bengal tigers can eat over 30 kg of meat in a single meal.
The Indian bullfrog male turns bright yellow during the breeding season.
The olive ridley is one of the smallest sea turtles.
India's Chilika Lake is home to Irrawaddy dolphins.
The barking deer gets its name from its alarm call, which sounds like a dog's bark.
The Indian grey mongoose can kill venomous snakes.
The spotted deer, or chital, often follows langur monkeys to pick up fruit they drop.
Langurs and chital also warn each other about tigers and leopards.
The blackbuck's twisted horns can grow over 60 cm long.
