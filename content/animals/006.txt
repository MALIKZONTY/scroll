# Animals — batch 6: pets, behaviour & animal intelligence
Dogs have been living with humans for at least 15,000 years.
Dogs can smell your emotions through chemical changes in your sweat, according to some studies.
Dogs can learn to understand pointing, something even chimpanzees find harder.
A dog's whiskers help it sense its surroundings.
Dogs dream, just like humans.
Puppies are born deaf and blind.
Dogs sweat mainly through their paw pads.
A Greyhound can run at about 70 km/h.
Indian pariah dogs, often called "Indies," are one of the oldest dog types in the world.
Indian street dogs are known for being smart, hardy and loyal.
Cats were domesticated about 10,000 years ago, possibly because they hunted mice in grain stores.
Cats sleep 12 to 16 hours a day.
Cats slow-blink to show trust. You can slow-blink back.
Cats knead with their paws when they're content, a habit from kittenhood.
A cat's whiskers are roughly as wide as its body, helping it judge gaps.
Cats can jump several times their own height.
Cats have a reflex that helps them land on their feet.
A group of kittens is called a kindle.
A cat's purr vibrates at around 25 to 150 hertz.
Cats can recognise their owners' voices.
Most cats are lactose intolerant, so milk isn't good for them.
Rabbits purr softly when happy.
Rabbits do a joyful jump-and-twist called a "binky."
Guinea pigs need vitamin C in their diet because, like humans, they can't make it.
Goldfish can learn to recognise their owners.
Goldfish can be trained to do simple tricks.
Budgies can learn to talk, sometimes with large vocabularies.
Parrots can live longer than their owners.
Hamsters can run several kilometres in one night on a wheel.
Horses can recognise human facial expressions.
Horses communicate with their ears, eyes and nostrils.
Cows produce more milk when they listen to calm music, according to a small study.
Pigs are smarter than many dogs in some tests.
Chickens can recognise dozens of other chickens' faces.
Chickens are descendants of the red junglefowl, which still lives wild in India.
Sheep can remember dozens of faces for years.
Goats were one of the first animals domesticated by humans.
Elephants can remember water sources and routes for decades.
Elephants show empathy and comfort others in distress.
Dolphins have been seen helping injured companions to the surface.
Orcas have been observed passing down hunting techniques across generations.
Chimpanzees can remember number sequences better than many humans in some tests.
Some apes have learned to use hundreds of signs or symbols to communicate.
Octopuses can open jars from the inside.
Octopuses have escaped from aquarium tanks to raid neighbouring ones.
Crows hold "funerals," gathering around dead crows, possibly to learn about danger.
Crows can understand water displacement, dropping stones into water to raise the level, like in Aesop's fable.
Ravens can imitate human speech.
Pigeons can tell the difference between paintings by Monet and Picasso, in experiments.
Bees can count up to four in experiments.
Bees can learn to play a simple ball game in experiments.
Ants can find their way home by counting steps.
Honeybees "vote" on new nest sites with dances.
Some fish use tools, like using rocks to crack shellfish.
Cleaner wrasse fish passed a version of the mirror test.
Rats show empathy, freeing trapped companions in experiments.
Rats can be trained to detect landmines and tuberculosis.
Sniffer dogs are used at airports to detect drugs, explosives and even some diseases.
Elephants in India have been seen making their own fly swatters from branches.
Langurs are sometimes used in Indian cities to scare away other monkeys.
Animals can sense earthquakes' early vibrations before humans feel them, though reliable prediction hasn't been proven.
Migrating birds may use Earth's magnetic field to navigate.
Sea turtles use Earth's magnetic field to find their way back to their birth beach.
Salmon return to the river where they were born to spawn, guided partly by smell.
Some butterflies use the Sun as a compass.
Homing pigeons can find their way back from hundreds of kilometres away.
Animals play. Ravens slide down snowy roofs, apparently for fun.
Otters juggle pebbles, possibly for fun or practice.
Young elephants play by chasing birds and splashing in water.
Dogs "play bow" to signal that rough play is just a game.
Many animals yawn, including fish and birds.
Some animals, like chimps, may find yawning contagious too.
Gorillas hum and sing while eating.
Humpback whale songs can spread across an entire ocean's population in a few years.
Prairie dogs have complex calls that may describe the size and colour of a predator.
Vervet monkeys use different alarm calls for eagles, leopards and snakes.
Meerkats teach their young how to handle scorpions safely.
Some birds, like the drongo, fake alarm calls to steal food from other animals.
