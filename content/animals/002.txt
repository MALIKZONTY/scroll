# Animals — batch 2: ocean life
The blue whale's heartbeat can be heard from kilometres away by sensitive underwater equipment.
Sperm whales sleep vertically, floating upright in the water.
Sperm whales have the largest brain of any animal.
Humpback whales sing complex songs that can change over time and spread across populations.
Orcas, also called killer whales, are actually the largest members of the dolphin family.
Orca pods have their own dialects of calls.
Narwhal tusks are actually long teeth that can grow over 2.5 metres.
Belugas are sometimes called "sea canaries" because of their chirps and whistles.
Dolphins have been seen using sponges to protect their noses while searching the seafloor for food.
Some dolphins sleep with one eye open.
Manta rays have one of the largest brain-to-body ratios of any fish.
Whale sharks are the biggest fish in the sea, but they eat tiny plankton.
Great white sharks can sense tiny electrical fields from other animals.
Sharks don't have bones. Their skeletons are made of cartilage, like your ears and nose.
Some sharks must keep swimming to breathe.
Greenland sharks can live for hundreds of years, possibly more than 400.
The Greenland shark is thought to be the longest-living vertebrate known.
Hammerhead sharks' wide heads help them sense prey and turn sharply.
A shark can lose thousands of teeth in its lifetime. New ones keep growing in.
Octopuses can change colour and texture in a fraction of a second.
Octopuses can squeeze through any gap bigger than their beak.
Most of an octopus's neurons are in its arms, not its head.
Some octopuses have been seen carrying coconut shells to use as shelters.
The mimic octopus can imitate other animals, like lionfish and sea snakes.
Squid and octopuses squirt ink to escape predators.
The giant squid has the largest eyes in the animal kingdom, about the size of a dinner plate.
The colossal squid is the heaviest known invertebrate.
Cuttlefish can see polarised light, which humans can't.
Jellyfish have no brain, no heart and no bones.
Some jellyfish glow in the dark.
The box jellyfish is one of the most venomous animals on Earth.
The lion's mane jellyfish can have tentacles longer than a blue whale.
Coral may look like a plant, but it's an animal.
Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor but support about a quarter of all marine species.
Clownfish are all born male. The dominant one can turn female.
Clownfish live safely among sea anemone tentacles, protected by a coating of mucus.
Parrotfish crunch coral and poop out fine white sand. Some tropical beaches are partly parrotfish poop.
Some parrotfish sleep inside a bubble of their own mucus.
Pufferfish can inflate to several times their normal size.
Many pufferfish contain a toxin far more poisonous than cyanide.
Seahorses swim upright and are quite bad swimmers.
Seahorse couples often greet each other each morning with a little dance.
Flying fish can glide through the air for over 200 metres.
The sailfish is one of the fastest fish in the ocean.
Electric eels aren't true eels. They're more closely related to catfish.
An electric eel can produce a shock of several hundred volts.
Anglerfish use a glowing lure on their heads to attract prey in the deep sea.
In some anglerfish species, the tiny male permanently fuses to the female.
The deep sea is home to animals that make their own light, called bioluminescence.
Sea cucumbers can push out their internal organs to scare predators, then regrow them.
Starfish eat by pushing their stomach out of their body and onto their prey.
Sea urchins move using hundreds of tiny tube feet.
Sea otters have the densest fur of any animal, up to about a million hairs per square inch.
Sea otters keep a favourite rock in a pouch under their arm to crack open shellfish.
Walruses use their tusks to haul themselves onto ice.
Seals can sleep underwater, surfacing to breathe without fully waking.
Elephant seals can dive more than 1.5 km deep.
Leatherback turtles can dive over 1,000 metres deep.
Sea turtles have been around for over 100 million years.
The temperature of the sand decides whether sea turtle eggs hatch as males or females.
Lobsters were once so common in parts of North America that they were considered poor people's food.
Lobsters taste with their legs.
Crabs can walk sideways faster than forwards.
Hermit crabs swap shells, and sometimes line up by size to trade.
The mantis shrimp's punch is one of the fastest strikes in the animal kingdom.
Mantis shrimp have far more types of colour-detecting cells in their eyes than humans.
Krill are tiny, but together they form one of the biggest masses of animals on Earth.
Penguins can drink seawater. A gland above their eyes filters out the salt.
Emperor penguins can dive over 500 metres deep.
Some penguins can swim faster than 30 km/h.
Albatrosses can fly for hours without flapping their wings.
The wandering albatross has the largest wingspan of any living bird, up to about 3.5 metres.
Some albatrosses spend years at sea without touching land.
Oysters can change sex during their lives.
Pearls form when an oyster coats an irritant with layers of nacre.
Blue whales can eat several tonnes of krill in a single day.
Dolphins can recognise themselves in a mirror.
The ocean sunfish is one of the heaviest bony fish, weighing over a tonne.
A female ocean sunfish can produce hundreds of millions of eggs at once.
Some fish, like gobies and shrimp, live together as partners: the shrimp digs and the fish keeps watch.
The vampire squid lives in the deep sea and isn't really a squid or a vampire.
Lanternfish are among the most numerous vertebrates on Earth.
Many deep-sea fish are red, because red light doesn't reach that deep, making them invisible.
