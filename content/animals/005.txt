# Animals — batch 5: reptiles, amphibians & weird animals
Crocodiles have been around for about 200 million years in some form.
Saltwater crocodiles are the largest living reptiles.
Crocodiles can't stick out their tongues.
Crocodiles swallow stones, which may help with digestion or balance in water.
India's gharial has a long, thin snout perfect for catching fish.
Male gharials have a bulbous growth on their snout called a ghara, named after a pot.
The king cobra is the world's longest venomous snake and is found in India.
King cobras mostly eat other snakes.
King cobras are the only snakes known to build nests for their eggs.
India is home to the "big four" venomous snakes: the cobra, the krait, the Russell's viper and the saw-scaled viper.
Snakes don't have eyelids.
Snakes can't hear airborne sounds well, but they sense vibrations through the ground.
Snake charmers' cobras respond to the movement of the pungi, not the music.
Some snakes can go months without eating.
Pythons can swallow prey larger than their heads because their jaws are flexible.
Sea snakes can breathe partly through their skin.
Flying snakes in Southeast Asia glide between trees by flattening their bodies.
Chameleons change colour mostly to communicate and regulate temperature, not just to camouflage.
Chameleons' eyes can move independently, looking in two directions at once.
A chameleon's tongue can shoot out in a fraction of a second.
Geckos can walk on ceilings thanks to millions of tiny hairs on their toes.
Some lizards can drop their tails to escape predators, and grow them back.
The Komodo dragon is the largest living lizard.
Komodo dragons can reproduce without a mate in rare cases.
Turtles can't leave their shells. The shell is part of their skeleton.
Tortoises can live for well over 100 years.
Jonathan, a tortoise on St Helena, is believed to be the oldest known living land animal, born around 1832.
Some turtles can breathe through their rear end, absorbing oxygen from water.
The Indian star tortoise has beautiful star patterns on its shell.
Frogs swallow using their eyes, pushing food down by retracting them.
Some frogs are so poisonous that touching them can be dangerous.
Glass frogs have see-through bellies. You can see their hearts beating.
The wood frog can survive being frozen, with its heart stopping until it thaws.
India's purple frog spends most of its life underground, coming up briefly during the monsoon.
Several frog species in the Western Ghats were discovered only in recent decades.
Some frogs carry their tadpoles on their backs.
Salamanders can regrow legs, tails and parts of their eyes.
The Chinese giant salamander can grow to nearly 2 metres long.
Caecilians are legless amphibians that look like large worms.
The platypus detects prey using electricity sensors in its bill.
The aye-aye taps on trees and listens for grubs inside, then fishes them out with a long finger.
Naked mole-rats hardly feel some kinds of pain and rarely get cancer.
Naked mole-rats can survive for minutes without oxygen.
Pangolins are the only mammals covered in scales.
Pangolins roll into a ball to protect themselves.
The Indian pangolin is found across much of India.
Armadillos can hold their breath for several minutes and walk underwater.
Hagfish produce huge amounts of slime when threatened.
The axolotl stays in its larval form its whole life.
The blobfish only looks blobby when brought to the surface. In the deep sea, it looks like a normal fish.
The shoebill stork looks prehistoric and can stand still for hours waiting for prey.
The okapi looks like a mix of a zebra and a giraffe, but it's a giraffe's closest living relative.
The saiga antelope has a large, bendy nose that filters dust and warms cold air.
The proboscis monkey has a huge nose, which males may use to make louder calls.
The star-nosed mole has 22 fleshy tentacles on its nose that help it find food incredibly fast.
The narwhal's tusk is full of nerve endings and may sense changes in the water.
The leafy sea dragon looks like floating seaweed.
The pistol shrimp snaps its claw so fast it creates a bubble that briefly reaches very high temperatures.
Mantis shrimp can see polarised light.
The lyrebird's song can include sounds of chainsaws and car alarms.
The cassowary is considered one of the most dangerous birds, with dagger-like claws.
The kakapo is a flightless, nocturnal parrot from New Zealand.
The secretary bird kicks snakes to death with powerful legs.
Pufferfish create beautiful circular patterns in the sand to attract mates.
Archerfish shoot jets of water to knock insects off leaves.
Some fish, like mudskippers, can walk on land.
The lungfish can breathe air and survive in dried-up mud for months.
The tuatara of New Zealand has a "third eye" on top of its head.
Thorny devils in Australia drink water through grooves in their skin.
The horned lizard can squirt blood from its eyes to scare predators.
The Indian flying lizard, Draco, glides using flaps of skin like wings.
India's Malabar gliding frog glides between trees using its large webbed feet.
Slow lorises are among the few venomous primates.
Some bats glow under ultraviolet light, as do platypuses and some other mammals.
Owl monkeys are the only nocturnal monkeys.
The tarsier has eyes larger than its brain.
The fossa of Madagascar looks like a cat but is related to mongooses.
