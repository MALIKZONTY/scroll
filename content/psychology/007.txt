# Psychology — batch 7: top-up
People tend to underestimate how much time tasks take, especially familiar ones.
We rate items higher when they're scarce or in limited stock.
The more effort we put into something, the more we tend to value it.
People are more likely to finish a goal as they get closer to it. It's called the goal-gradient effect.
Customers buy coffee faster as they get closer to a free one on a loyalty card.
We remember things better when we're in the same place or mood where we learned them.
Chewing gum while studying and during an exam may help some people remember, but the effect is small.
Your brain notices faces faster than almost any other object.
People can spot an angry face in a crowd faster than a happy one.
Babies prefer looking at faces from birth.
A baby's cry is hard to ignore because our brains are wired to respond to it.
People are generally better at remembering pictures than words.
Drawing what you want to remember can boost memory more than writing it.
Saying information out loud helps you remember it. It's called the production effect.
We often remember the gist of a conversation, not the exact words.
Repeating a lie can make people believe it more, even if they knew the truth at first.
People are more likely to believe a statement printed in an easy-to-read font.
Feeling uncertain can make people cling more tightly to their beliefs.
Curiosity can make information more memorable, even unrelated facts learned at the same time.
Pretending to be confident can sometimes lead to real confidence over time.
People feel more in control when they have even small choices.
Giving children choices, like "red cup or blue cup?", can reduce tantrums.
Anticipating a reward can feel as good as receiving it.
The brain's reward system responds strongly to surprises.
Unexpected rewards feel better than expected ones.
People are often happier with experiences they almost didn't have.
Shared experiences feel more intense than solo ones, according to one study.
Eating chocolate with someone else can make it taste better, in one experiment.
Laughing at yourself can make you more likeable.
People who can laugh at their mistakes tend to bounce back faster.
Being curious about other people makes you more interesting to them.
People remember stories with emotion far better than plain facts.
