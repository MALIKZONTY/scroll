# Earth — batch 1 (well-established facts only)
More than 80% of the ocean has never been mapped in detail, explored or even seen by humans.
Earth isn't a perfect ball. It bulges at the equator because it's spinning.
Because of that bulge, the peak of Ecuador's Chimborazo is the point on Earth farthest from its centre, not Everest.
About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water.
Around 97% of Earth's water is salty ocean water. Most of the fresh water is locked in ice.
The Mariana Trench is about 11 km deep. Mount Everest would fit inside with room to spare.
Mount Everest grows a few millimetres taller every year as the Indian plate keeps pushing into Asia.
The Himalayas exist because India crashed into Asia. The collision started about 50 million years ago and is still going.
Antarctica is the largest desert on Earth. A desert is defined by low precipitation, not heat.
The Sahara is the largest hot desert, about the size of the United States.
The Amazon rainforest produces a huge share of its own rain by releasing water vapour from its trees.
Lightning strikes Earth about 40 to 50 times every second.
A single bolt of lightning can be about five times hotter than the surface of the Sun.
Earth's inner core is about as hot as the surface of the Sun, around 5,000 °C or more.
Earth's inner core is solid, even though it's incredibly hot, because the pressure is so enormous.
Earth is the only planet not named after a god.
The Pacific Ocean is bigger than all of Earth's land put together.
The Dead Sea is so salty that you can float on it without trying.
The shore of the Dead Sea is the lowest land point on Earth, more than 400 metres below sea level.
The continents are still moving, about as fast as your fingernails grow.
About 250 million years ago, all the continents were joined in one supercontinent called Pangaea.
Earth's magnetic field has flipped many times. The last full flip happened about 780,000 years ago.
The aurora is caused by particles from the Sun hitting Earth's atmosphere and making the air glow.
Earth is about 4.5 billion years old.
The oldest rocks found on Earth are about 4 billion years old.
Tiny zircon crystals from Australia are about 4.4 billion years old, the oldest known pieces of Earth.
The Great Barrier Reef is the largest structure made by living things, and it's visible from space.
The Great Wall of China is not visible from the Moon with the naked eye. That's a myth.
Mawsynram and Cherrapunji in Meghalaya are among the wettest places on Earth.
Earth's rotation is gradually slowing down. Days were much shorter in the age of the dinosaurs.
About 1.4 billion years ago, a day on Earth may have lasted only around 18 to 19 hours.
Over 75% of Earth's active volcanoes are thought to be underwater.
The Pacific "Ring of Fire" holds about 75% of the world's volcanoes.
Iceland sits right on top of a split between two tectonic plates. You can snorkel between them at Silfra.
The longest mountain range on Earth is underwater: the mid-ocean ridge, about 65,000 km long.
The Atacama Desert in Chile has places where no rain has ever been recorded.
The hottest air temperature officially recorded on Earth is 56.7 °C, in Death Valley, USA, in 1913.
The coldest temperature recorded on Earth's surface is −89.2 °C, at Vostok Station in Antarctica.
Earth travels around the Sun at about 107,000 km/h.
Even standing still at the equator, you're spinning with Earth at about 1,670 km/h.
Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during the northern winter. Seasons come from the tilt, not the distance.
Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.4 degrees. That tilt gives us seasons.
The Nile and the Amazon are the two longest rivers in the world. Which one is longer is still debated.
The Amazon carries more water than any other river, roughly a fifth of all river water reaching the oceans.
The Ganges delta, shared by India and Bangladesh, is the largest river delta in the world.
The Sundarbans is the largest mangrove forest on Earth and home to the Bengal tiger.
Lake Baikal in Russia holds about 20% of the world's unfrozen fresh surface water.
Lake Baikal is the deepest lake on Earth, over 1,600 metres deep.
Angel Falls in Venezuela is the tallest waterfall in the world, nearly 1 km high.
Russia spans 11 time zones.
Canada has the longest coastline of any country.
Greenland is the world's largest island that isn't a continent.
Earth's atmosphere is mostly nitrogen, about 78%. Oxygen is about 21%.
The ozone layer protects life from most of the Sun's harmful UV rays.
Most of the oxygen we breathe from the ocean comes from tiny plankton, not trees.
Antarctica's ice sheet is up to about 4.8 km thick in places.
If all of Antarctica's ice melted, sea levels would rise by about 58 metres.
Old ice cores from Antarctica trap tiny air bubbles that are hundreds of thousands of years old.
A cloud can weigh hundreds of tonnes, even though it floats.
Raindrops aren't tear-shaped. Small ones are round, and big ones are shaped more like a hamburger bun.
No two snowflakes are thought to be exactly alike, because each one forms along a slightly different path.
The deepest hole humans ever dug, the Kola Superdeep Borehole, reached about 12.2 km. That's still only a tiny scratch on Earth's crust.
Earth's crust is thinnest under the oceans, only around 5 to 10 km thick.
Earthquakes happen constantly. Seismographs record hundreds of thousands of them each year, most too small to feel.
The 1960 Chile earthquake was the strongest ever recorded, magnitude 9.5.
Some sand dunes "sing," making a deep humming sound when sand slides down them.
Earth has a thin layer of space dust falling on it every day, tens of tonnes of it.
The Thar Desert in Rajasthan is one of the most densely populated deserts in the world.
India has some of the world's oldest mountain ranges too. The Aravallis are over a billion years old.
The Deccan Traps in India were formed by massive volcanic eruptions around 66 million years ago, near the time the dinosaurs died out.
The Kaziranga National Park in Assam is home to about two-thirds of the world's greater one-horned rhinos.
There are rainbow-coloured mountains in China's Zhangye Danxia area, made of layered sandstone.
Hawaii's Mauna Kea is taller than Everest if you measure from its base on the ocean floor.
Earth's average distance from the Sun is about 150 million kilometres.
A day on Earth is actually about 23 hours and 56 minutes long, measured against the stars.
Earth is the densest planet in the solar system.
Earth's oceans contain nearly 20 million tonnes of dissolved gold, but it's far too diluted to collect.
