# Earth — batch 7: top-up
Earth's oceans hold about 1.3 billion cubic kilometres of water.
If Earth were smooth, water would cover it to a depth of about 2.7 km.
The Atlantic Ocean is saltier than the Pacific.
The Arctic is the smallest and shallowest of the world's five oceans.
The Southern Ocean around Antarctica was officially recognised as the fifth ocean by some agencies only recently.
The Antarctic Circumpolar Current is the strongest ocean current on Earth.
Ocean waves can travel thousands of kilometres before breaking on a beach.
The biggest wave ever surfed was over 26 metres tall, at Nazaré in Portugal.
Rogue waves, once thought to be myths, have been measured by scientists.
Seawater freezes at about −2 °C because of its salt.
Sea ice is mostly fresh water, because salt is pushed out as it freezes.
The ocean absorbs about a quarter of the carbon dioxide humans release.
Kerala's backwaters stretch for hundreds of kilometres.
The Arabian Sea gets more cyclones in recent decades than it used to, according to some studies.
The Bay of Bengal produces some of the world's deadliest cyclones.
The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami struck 14 countries, including India.
India issues cyclone names in rotation with other countries in the region.
Cyclone names are chosen in advance from lists proposed by countries around the Indian Ocean.
Weather forecasts are far more accurate today than 30 years ago.
A five-day forecast today is about as accurate as a one-day forecast was in 1980.
Satellites, supercomputers and weather balloons all help forecast the weather.
India's Meteorological Department was founded in 1875.
Mausam is the Hindi word for weather, and it comes from the same root as "monsoon."
The southwest monsoon usually reaches Kerala around the first of June.
The monsoon covers the whole of India by early to mid-July in a normal year.
The northeast monsoon brings much of Tamil Nadu's rain, in October to December.
Western disturbances bring winter rain and snow to north India.
Loo is the hot, dry summer wind that blows across north India.
Mango showers are pre-monsoon rains in Kerala and Karnataka that help mangoes ripen.
Kalbaisakhi, or nor'westers, are fierce spring thunderstorms in eastern India.
Fog in north India's winter can delay flights and trains for hours.
Shimla, Manali and Gulmarg get regular snowfall in winter.
Drass in Ladakh is one of the coldest inhabited places in India.
Phalodi in Rajasthan recorded 51 °C in 2016, one of India's highest temperatures.
Leh's air holds less oxygen than at sea level because of its altitude.
At high altitude, water boils at a lower temperature, so cooking takes longer.
Pressure cookers became popular partly because they cook faster at any altitude.
Hailstorms can damage crops in minutes.
Dust storms in Rajasthan can turn the sky orange.
India has 15 agro-climatic zones.
Black soil, found in the Deccan, is great for growing cotton.
Red soil gets its colour from iron.
Alluvial soil, deposited by rivers, covers much of north India's plains.
Laterite soil is used to make bricks in parts of Kerala and Karnataka.
