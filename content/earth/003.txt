# Earth — batch 3: countries, places & geography
Russia is the largest country by area, spanning both Europe and Asia.
Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, at less than half a square kilometre.
India is the seventh-largest country by area.
The Indian Ocean is the only ocean named after a country.
The Tropic of Cancer passes through eight Indian states.
India's southernmost point, Indira Point, is in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
Kanyakumari is where the Arabian Sea, the Bay of Bengal and the Indian Ocean meet.
At Kanyakumari, on some days, you can watch the sun rise and set over the sea.
Arunachal Pradesh gets one of India's earliest sunrises.
Ladakh is a cold desert, with some places getting very little rainfall.
Chilika Lake in Odisha is the largest coastal lagoon in India.
The Andaman Islands have India's only active volcano, on Barren Island.
Lakshadweep is made of coral islands.
The Thar Desert spreads across Rajasthan and into Pakistan.
The Godavari is the longest river in peninsular India.
Mount Everest sits on the border of Nepal and China (Tibet).
Everest's height was officially updated to 8,848.86 metres in 2020.
Nepal's flag is the only national flag that isn't a rectangle.
Bhutan measures its success with Gross National Happiness.
Bhutan is often described as carbon-negative, absorbing more carbon than it emits.
Indonesia has more than 17,000 islands.
The Philippines has more than 7,000 islands.
Australia is both a country and a continent.
Africa has 54 countries, more than any other continent.
Africa is the only continent that lies in all four hemispheres.
Canada has more lakes than the rest of the world combined, by some counts.
Finland has around 188,000 lakes.
Norway's fjords were carved by glaciers.
Ireland has no native snakes.
New Zealand has more sheep than people.
Monaco is so small and dense that it has almost no farmland at all.
Mongolia is the least densely populated country in the world.
Bangladesh is one of the most densely populated countries on Earth.
Brazil is home to most of the Amazon rainforest.
Chile is over 4,000 km long but, on average, only about 175 km wide.
Peru's Lake Titicaca, shared with Bolivia, is one of the highest navigable lakes in the world.
Bolivia's Salar de Uyuni is the largest salt flat on Earth. After rain, it becomes a giant mirror.
La Paz in Bolivia is one of the highest capital cities in the world, at about 3,600 metres.
Ecuador is named after the equator, which runs through it.
Mexico City is slowly sinking because of groundwater being pumped out.
Venice is built on more than 100 small islands connected by bridges.
Istanbul is one of the few cities that sits on two continents, Europe and Asia.
Egypt's population mostly lives along the Nile, on a small fraction of the country's land.
The Sahara was green with lakes and grasslands thousands of years ago.
The Dead Sea is shrinking, dropping by around a metre a year.
Japan has thousands of earthquakes every year.
Japan is made up of more than 14,000 islands, according to a recent count.
Singapore is both a city and a country.
China uses just one time zone, despite being about as wide as the USA.
The USA and Russia are only about 4 km apart at their closest, across the Bering Strait.
Alaska was bought by the USA from Russia in 1867.
Greenland is mostly covered by ice, even though its name suggests otherwise.
Antarctica has no permanent human residents, only researchers who rotate.
Antarctica contains about 90% of the world's ice.
The South Pole sits on a sheet of ice about 2.7 km thick.
The North Pole has no land underneath. It's floating sea ice.
Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake by area.
The Caspian Sea is the largest inland body of water on Earth, and it's actually a lake.
The Amazon River has no bridges across its main stretch.
The Danube flows through or along the borders of 10 countries, more than any other river.
The Mekong flows through six countries in Asia.
Mount Everest isn't the tallest mountain from base to peak. Mauna Kea in Hawaii is.
Mount Chimborazo in Ecuador is the closest point on Earth to the Moon and the stars, because of Earth's bulge.
The Andes are the longest mountain range above water, running down South America.
The Alps stretch across eight countries.
Scotland has more than 790 islands.
The Netherlands has a large part of its land below sea level, protected by dikes.
Bangkok's full ceremonial name is one of the longest place names in the world.
Australia's Great Dividing Range is one of the longest mountain ranges on land.
Uluru in Australia is a giant sandstone monolith, sacred to the Aboriginal Anangu people.
The Gobi Desert is expanding, partly because of climate and land use.
The Arabian Peninsula is the largest peninsula in the world.
The Indian subcontinent was once an island drifting north across the ocean.
