# Earth — batch 2: weather, oceans & natural wonders
A hurricane, a cyclone and a typhoon are the same kind of storm. The name changes with the ocean.
The eye of a cyclone is calm, often with clear skies, while the strongest winds swirl around it.
Cyclones spin anticlockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
India's monsoon brings most of the country's yearly rainfall in about four months.
The word "monsoon" comes from the Arabic word "mawsim," meaning season.
The monsoon happens because land heats up faster than the sea, pulling in moist ocean air.
Rainbows are actually full circles. From the ground, we usually see only the top half.
A double rainbow has its colours reversed in the second, outer arc.
You can only see a rainbow when the Sun is behind you.
Fog is basically a cloud touching the ground.
Thunder is the sound of air expanding explosively when lightning heats it.
You can estimate how far away lightning is by counting the seconds until thunder. About 3 seconds is 1 km.
Hailstones can grow bigger than tennis balls.
The largest hailstone recorded in the USA was about 20 cm across.
Tornadoes can have wind speeds over 400 km/h.
The USA gets more tornadoes than any other country.
Snow isn't white. Each crystal is clear, but the way it scatters light makes it look white.
Lightning can happen in snowstorms. It's called thundersnow.
Some deserts get snow, including the Sahara on rare occasions.
The Gulf Stream carries warm water across the Atlantic, keeping western Europe milder than it would otherwise be.
Ocean currents act like a giant conveyor belt, moving heat around the planet.
Tides are mainly caused by the Moon's gravity, with help from the Sun.
The Bay of Fundy in Canada has some of the highest tides on Earth, up to about 16 metres.
Most places on Earth get two high tides and two low tides a day.
A tsunami can travel across an ocean at the speed of a jet plane.
In deep water, a tsunami may be less than a metre high, but it grows huge when it reaches shallow coasts.
The ocean's average depth is about 3.7 km.
Sunlight barely reaches below 200 metres in the ocean. Below 1,000 metres, it's completely dark.
The pressure at the bottom of the Mariana Trench is over 1,000 times the pressure at the surface.
Only a few people have ever been to the bottom of the Challenger Deep.
The ocean is salty mainly because rivers carry dissolved minerals from rocks into the sea.
If the ocean's salt were spread over land, it would form a layer over 150 metres thick.
The Red Sea is one of the saltiest seas connected to the ocean.
Hot springs and geysers happen where underground water is heated by magma.
Old Faithful, a geyser in Yellowstone, erupts roughly every hour or so.
Yellowstone sits on top of a massive volcanic hotspot.
Some volcanoes make their own lightning, called volcanic lightning or a dirty thunderstorm.
The 1815 eruption of Tambora in Indonesia was so huge it caused the "Year Without a Summer" in 1816.
Krakatoa's 1883 eruption was so loud it was heard thousands of kilometres away.
Volcanic soil is often very fertile, which is why many people farm near volcanoes.
Iceland gets a large share of its electricity and heating from geothermal energy.
Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania is the highest free-standing mountain in the world.
K2 is the second-highest mountain on Earth and is considered much harder to climb than Everest.
Kangchenjunga, on the India-Nepal border, is the third-highest mountain in the world.
The Himalayas contain the world's highest peaks, including all ten of the tallest.
The Siachen Glacier in the Karakoram is one of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions.
The Ganges begins at the Gangotri Glacier in the Himalayas.
The Brahmaputra is one of the few Indian rivers usually given a male name.
Majuli in Assam is one of the largest river islands in the world.
Dal Lake in Srinagar has floating gardens and floating markets.
Lonar Lake in Maharashtra was formed by a meteorite impact, possibly over half a million years ago.
The Western Ghats are one of the world's biodiversity hotspots.
Bioluminescent plankton can make ocean waves glow blue at night, a sight seen on some Indian beaches.
The Rann of Kutch turns into a vast white salt desert in the dry season.
Victoria Falls is often called "the smoke that thunders" because of its huge spray.
Niagara Falls has slowly moved upstream over thousands of years as rock erodes.
The Grand Canyon was carved over millions of years by the Colorado River.
The Great Rift Valley in East Africa is slowly splitting the African continent apart.
The Amazon rainforest covers parts of nine countries.
The Congo rainforest is the second-largest tropical rainforest on Earth.
Tropical rainforests cover a small part of Earth's surface but hold more than half of land plant and animal species.
Some trees in the Amazon are taller than 80 metres.
The tallest known living tree is a coast redwood called Hyperion, over 115 metres tall.
Some bristlecone pines in California are over 4,000 years old.
Pando, a grove of aspen trees in Utah, is actually one organism with a shared root system.
A single large tree can release hundreds of litres of water into the air on a hot day.
Bamboo can grow almost a metre in a single day.
Peat bogs store huge amounts of carbon.
Permafrost is ground that stays frozen for at least two years in a row.
Glaciers store about 69% of the world's fresh water.
Glaciers move, slowly flowing downhill like very thick rivers.
Icebergs float with about 90% of their mass below the water.
The iceberg that sank the Titanic probably came from Greenland.
The Northern Lights are called the aurora borealis. The Southern Lights are the aurora australis.
In parts of the Arctic, the Sun doesn't set for weeks in summer.
In winter, places above the Arctic Circle can go weeks without sunrise.
The equator is the only place where day and night are close to 12 hours each all year.
Earth's highest recorded wind gust outside a tornado was about 408 km/h, on Barrow Island, Australia, during a cyclone.
