# Time & universe — batch 3: physics & weirdness
In quantum physics, particles can behave like waves and waves like particles.
In the double-slit experiment, single particles create an interference pattern, as if they went through both slits.
Quantum entanglement links particles so that measuring one instantly tells you about the other, no matter how far apart they are.
Einstein called entanglement "spooky action at a distance."
Entanglement can't be used to send messages faster than light.
Heisenberg's uncertainty principle says you can't know both a particle's exact position and its exact momentum.
Quantum tunnelling lets particles pass through barriers they "shouldn't" be able to cross. It helps power the Sun.
Without quantum tunnelling, the Sun's core wouldn't be hot enough for fusion to happen at its current rate.
Empty space isn't truly empty. It's filled with fleeting quantum fluctuations.
Schrödinger's cat was a thought experiment meant to show how strange quantum ideas seem at everyday scales.
Electrons don't orbit atoms like tiny planets. They exist in fuzzy clouds of probability.
Atoms are mostly empty space, but the electromagnetic force stops you from passing through walls.
When you touch something, your atoms never truly touch its atoms. Electric forces push them apart.
Every atom of gold, oxygen or carbon is identical to every other atom of the same type.
The speed of light is exactly 299,792,458 metres per second. The metre is defined by it.
Light slows down in water and glass. That's why things look bent in a glass of water.
Nothing can travel faster than light through space, but space itself can expand faster.
A photon of light doesn't experience time, according to relativity.
If you travelled near the speed of light for a while and came back, more time would have passed on Earth than for you.
This "twin paradox" has been tested with atomic clocks flown on aeroplanes.
Muons created in the upper atmosphere reach the ground only because time dilation lets them live longer than expected.
Your head ages slightly faster than your feet, because gravity is a tiny bit weaker higher up.
The difference between your head and feet ageing over a lifetime is less than a millionth of a second.
Mass and energy are two forms of the same thing.
Most of the mass of an atom comes from the energy binding its quarks together, not from the quarks themselves.
The Higgs boson, discovered in 2012, is linked to how particles get mass.
The Large Hadron Collider is a 27 km ring buried under the border of France and Switzerland.
The LHC is one of the coldest places in the universe, cooled to about −271 °C.
Antimatter is real. When it meets regular matter, both are destroyed in a burst of energy.
Hospitals use antimatter every day in PET scans.
Physicists still don't know why the universe has more matter than antimatter.
Bananas are slightly radioactive because they contain potassium.
You're slightly radioactive too, for the same reason.
There are four known fundamental forces: gravity, electromagnetism, the strong force and the weak force.
Gravity is by far the weakest force. A small magnet can lift a paperclip against the whole Earth's gravity.
Nobody has yet joined gravity and quantum physics into one theory.
String theory suggests that the smallest building blocks might be tiny vibrating strings, but it hasn't been proven.
Some theories propose that our universe might be one of many, a multiverse. There's no evidence yet.
Time might be made of tiny units, but nobody knows. The smallest meaningful time is thought to be the Planck time.
The Planck time is about 10⁻⁴³ seconds, unimaginably short.
A second contains more Planck times than the age of the universe contains seconds, by a gigantic margin.
Physics equations mostly work the same forwards and backwards in time. Yet we only experience one direction.
Ice melts, eggs break and rooms get messy because disorder, called entropy, tends to increase.
The universe started in a state of very low entropy, and nobody knows why.
Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air.
Sound can't travel through space, but light can.
Plasma is the most common state of visible matter in the universe. Stars are made of it.
Glass is an amorphous solid, with atoms arranged randomly like a frozen liquid.
Water expands when it freezes, which is why ice floats.
If ice didn't float, lakes would freeze from the bottom up and much life couldn't survive winter.
Diamond and graphite, the stuff in pencils, are both pure carbon. Only their structure is different.
Helium can become a "superfluid" that climbs up the walls of its container.
At very low temperatures, some materials become superconductors with zero electrical resistance.
Superconducting magnets can make objects float in mid-air.
The Northern Lights happen when particles from the Sun hit our atmosphere, a bit like a neon sign.
A spoon in hot tea gets hot because fast-moving particles bump into slower ones and share energy.
Heat always flows from hot things to cold things, never the other way on its own.
A perfect perpetual motion machine is impossible because of the laws of thermodynamics.
Magnets work because of the way electrons spin and move.
Earth itself is a giant magnet, which is why compasses point north.
Earth's magnetic north pole is slowly wandering across the Arctic.
Radio waves, visible light and X-rays are all the same thing, electromagnetic waves, just with different wavelengths.
Humans can see only a tiny slice of the electromagnetic spectrum.
Bees can see ultraviolet light, which reveals hidden patterns on flowers.
Rainbows contain colours beyond what we can see, in infrared and ultraviolet.
The sky is blue because air scatters blue light from the Sun more than red light.
Sunsets are red and orange because sunlight travels through more air, scattering away the blue.
On the Moon, the sky is black even in daytime because there's no air to scatter light.
Clouds are white because water droplets scatter all colours of light equally.
A mirror doesn't actually flip left and right. It flips front and back.
The colour you see is the colour an object reflects, not the colour it absorbs.
Black objects get hotter in the sun because they absorb all colours of light.
Microwaves heat food by making water molecules jiggle.
Your phone's GPS relies on Einstein's theories to be accurate.
Solar panels work because light can knock electrons free, an effect Einstein explained. It won him the Nobel Prize.
Einstein won his Nobel Prize for explaining the photoelectric effect, not for relativity.
