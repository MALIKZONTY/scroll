# Time & universe — batch 2: scale & perspective
If the Sun were the size of a front door, Earth would be about the size of a coin.
If the Sun were a basketball in Delhi, the nearest star would be another basketball thousands of kilometres away.
The distance to the nearest star is about 40 trillion kilometres.
A spacecraft as fast as Voyager 1 would take over 70,000 years to reach the nearest star.
If you could drive a car to the Sun at 100 km/h, the trip would take about 170 years.
Driving to the Moon at 100 km/h would take about 160 days.
All the planets in the solar system could fit in the gap between Earth and the Moon.
The Milky Way contains an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars.
Our solar system takes about 230 million years to orbit the centre of the Milky Way. That's called a galactic year.
The last time the solar system was in its current spot in the galaxy, dinosaurs were just appearing.
The Sun is moving around the galaxy at roughly 800,000 km/h.
The Milky Way is part of a group of galaxies called the Local Group.
The Local Group is part of an even bigger structure called the Laniakea Supercluster.
Laniakea means "immeasurable heaven" in Hawaiian.
Galaxies are arranged in a cosmic web of filaments with huge empty voids between them.
Some cosmic voids are hundreds of millions of light-years across.
The most distant objects we can see are so far away that their light has been travelling for most of the universe's history.
The James Webb telescope's first deep field showed thousands of galaxies in a patch of sky about the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length.
The Moon is about 384,000 km away. You could fit about 30 Earths in that gap.
Light could travel from Earth to the Moon and back in about 2.6 seconds.
A radio signal from Mars takes between about 3 and 22 minutes to reach Earth, depending on where the planets are.
Because of that delay, Mars rovers can't be driven in real time with a joystick.
Signals from Voyager 1 now take more than 20 hours to reach Earth.
If Earth's history were squeezed into one hour, dinosaurs would appear in the last minute or so.
Humans have existed for less than 0.01% of Earth's history.
Writing was invented only about 5,000 years ago.
The age of the universe is about three times the age of Earth.
The Sun is a middle-aged star, and smaller than many other stars.
The biggest known stars, like UY Scuti or Stephenson 2-18, are so large they could swallow the orbit of Jupiter or beyond.
A red dwarf star can shine for trillions of years, far longer than the universe has existed.
Stars more massive than the Sun burn out much faster, sometimes in just a few million years.
The universe is so young that no red dwarf has ever died of old age yet.
A star's colour reveals its temperature. Blue stars are the hottest, red stars the coolest.
Betelgeuse, the red star in Orion, is a supergiant expected to explode as a supernova sometime in the next 100,000 years.
When a big star explodes as a supernova, it can briefly outshine its entire galaxy.
In 1054, a supernova was so bright that it was visible in daylight for weeks. Its leftovers are the Crab Nebula.
Most of the elements heavier than iron were made in supernovae or neutron star collisions.
Hydrogen and helium make up about 98% of all ordinary matter in the universe.
Hydrogen is the most common element in the universe.
Every hydrogen atom in your body was formed in the first few minutes after the Big Bang.
Some of the water in your glass may be older than the Sun.
Quasars are the brightest objects in the universe, powered by supermassive black holes eating matter.
Some quasars outshine their entire galaxy by thousands of times.
Galaxies collide all the time on cosmic timescales, but their stars rarely hit each other because space is so empty.
The space between stars is emptier than the best vacuum we can make on Earth.
The temperature of deep space is about −270 °C, set by the cosmic background glow.
A neutron star packs more mass than the Sun into a ball about the size of a city.
The heaviest black holes known are billions of times the mass of the Sun.
If the Earth were squeezed into a black hole, it would be about the size of a marble.
Time near a black hole's horizon would slow so much that a far-away observer would see you freeze in place.
The light we see from the Sun's surface is about 8 minutes old, but from Pluto the Sun's light is over 5 hours old.
Sunlight reaches Neptune after about 4 hours.
Earth is moving through space at many different speeds at once: spinning, orbiting the Sun, circling the galaxy and travelling with the galaxy itself.
You've travelled roughly 940 million kilometres around the Sun in the last year without feeling a thing.
There are more trees on Earth than stars in the Milky Way, according to some estimates: around 3 trillion trees.
The number of stars in the observable universe is often estimated at around 10²², or ten thousand billion billion.
A single grain of sand contains more atoms than there are stars in the Milky Way.
If you counted one star per second, it would take thousands of years just to count the stars in our galaxy.
The universe is about 46 billion light-years in every direction we can see, even though it's only 13.8 billion years old, because space has expanded.
Some galaxies we see today are now so far away that light they emit now will never reach us.
In the far future, galaxies outside our Local Group will drift beyond what we can see.
The cosmic microwave background is the oldest light in the universe, about 13.8 billion years old.
Our solar system formed from a giant cloud of gas and dust about 4.6 billion years ago.
Earth formed from countless collisions of rocks and dust in the young solar system.
Jupiter may have shaped the early solar system by moving inwards and then back out, according to one theory.
The Sun will eventually shrink into a white dwarf, about the size of Earth.
A white dwarf is so dense that a teaspoon of it could weigh several tonnes.
Planets have been found orbiting two suns at once, just like Tatooine in Star Wars.
Some exoplanets orbit their star in less than a day.
There are rogue planets that drift through space with no star at all.
The TRAPPIST-1 system has seven Earth-sized planets orbiting a small, cool star.
Proxima Centauri b is a planet orbiting the nearest star to the Sun.
The Drake equation is a way of estimating how many alien civilisations might exist. Nobody knows the right numbers to put in.
The Fermi paradox asks: if the universe is so big and old, where is everybody?
The Arecibo message, sent in 1974, will take about 25,000 years to reach its target star cluster.
