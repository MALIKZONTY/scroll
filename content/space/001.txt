# Space — batch 1 (well-established facts only; keep numbers rounded and conservative)
A day on Venus is longer than its year. It takes 243 Earth days to spin once, but only 225 to orbit the Sun.
A teaspoon of neutron star would weigh about a billion tonnes on Earth.
Venus spins backwards compared with most planets. On Venus, the Sun rises in the west.
Jupiter is so big that all the other planets in the solar system could fit inside it.
Saturn is less dense than water. In a big enough bathtub, it would float.
The footprints left on the Moon by Apollo astronauts could last millions of years. There's no wind to blow them away.
A year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days.
Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest volcano in the solar system, about two and a half times taller than Mount Everest.
The Sun makes up about 99.8% of all the mass in our solar system.
Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
There are more stars in the universe than grains of sand on all of Earth's beaches, by most estimates.
Neptune was the first planet found by maths. Astronomers predicted where it should be before anyone saw it.
Uranus is tipped over on its side. It basically rolls around the Sun.
One million Earths could fit inside the Sun.
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth that has been raging for centuries.
Space is completely silent. With no air to carry sound waves, there's nothing to hear.
Mars has two tiny moons, Phobos and Deimos. Their names mean "fear" and "dread."
Mars looks red because its surface is covered in iron oxide. Basically, the planet is rusty.
The International Space Station orbits Earth about every 90 minutes. Astronauts see around 16 sunrises a day.
The Moon is slowly drifting away from Earth, by about 3.8 centimetres a year.
We always see the same side of the Moon. It spins exactly once for every trip around Earth.
Our galaxy, the Milky Way, is about 100,000 light-years across.
The Andromeda Galaxy is on a collision course with the Milky Way. They're expected to merge in about 4 to 5 billion years.
Saturn's rings are mostly made of ice, with some rock and dust.
Saturn's rings are enormous but thin. In many places they're only about 10 metres thick.
A black hole's gravity is so strong that not even light can escape once it crosses the event horizon.
At the centre of our galaxy sits a supermassive black hole called Sagittarius A*. It's about 4 million times the mass of the Sun.
In 2019, scientists released the first-ever image of a black hole, in the galaxy M87.
The Voyager 1 probe, launched in 1977, is the most distant human-made object, more than 20 billion kilometres away.
Voyager 1 and 2 each carry a golden record with sounds and images from Earth, in case aliens ever find them.
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006.
Pluto has a giant heart-shaped plain on its surface, nicknamed Tombaugh Regio.
A day on Jupiter lasts only about 10 hours. It's the fastest-spinning planet.
Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun. Its thick atmosphere traps heat.
The surface of Venus is hot enough to melt lead.
Jupiter has dozens of moons. Ganymede, the largest, is bigger than the planet Mercury.
Saturn's moon Titan has lakes and rivers, but they're filled with liquid methane, not water.
Jupiter's moon Europa likely has a huge ocean of liquid water under its icy crust.
Saturn's moon Enceladus shoots jets of water vapour and ice into space from its south pole.
The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old, roughly halfway through its life.
In about 5 billion years, the Sun will swell into a red giant.
Neutron stars can spin hundreds of times per second.
The coldest known place in the universe is the Boomerang Nebula, at about −272 °C, just one degree above absolute zero.
Astronauts can grow up to about 5 centimetres taller in space because their spines stretch without gravity.
In microgravity, flames burn in a round ball instead of a teardrop shape.
The Moon has quakes too. Scientists call them moonquakes.
Mercury has almost no atmosphere, so its temperature swings from about 430 °C in the day to about −180 °C at night.
Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 km/h.
The Kármán line, about 100 km up, is commonly used as the boundary where space begins.
Light from the nearest star after the Sun, Proxima Centauri, takes about 4.2 years to reach us.
When you look at the stars, you're seeing them as they were when the light left them, sometimes thousands of years ago.
A light-year is a distance, not a time. It's how far light travels in a year: about 9.46 trillion kilometres.
Halley's Comet passes Earth about every 76 years. It's next due in 2061.
Shooting stars aren't stars. They're tiny bits of space dust burning up in our atmosphere.
Most meteors are no bigger than a grain of sand.
The Sun's core reaches about 15 million °C.
Sunlight reaching you today was made in the Sun's core long ago. Energy can take over 100,000 years to work its way out to the surface.
Jupiter has faint rings too. They're just much harder to see than Saturn's.
Every planet in our solar system except Mercury and Venus has at least one moon.
A full spacesuit for a NASA spacewalk is a mini spacecraft with its own oxygen, cooling and power.
The first animal to orbit Earth was a dog named Laika, in 1957.
Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space on 12 April 1961.
Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon on 20 July 1969.
Twelve people have walked on the Moon, all between 1969 and 1972.
Valentina Tereshkova became the first woman in space in 1963.
Rakesh Sharma became the first Indian in space in 1984. Asked how India looked from space, he said: "Saare Jahan Se Achha."
India's Chandrayaan-3 made the first soft landing near the Moon's south pole in August 2023.
India's Mangalyaan reached Mars orbit on its first attempt in 2014.
The Hubble Space Telescope has been orbiting Earth since 1990.
The James Webb Space Telescope sits about 1.5 million kilometres from Earth.
Mars has the biggest canyon in the solar system, Valles Marineris. It would stretch across most of the USA.
Mars has seasons, like Earth, because its axis is tilted by a similar amount.
Sunsets on Mars look blue.
Olympus Mons is so wide that if you stood on its edge, the summit would be beyond the horizon.
Comets have tails that always point away from the Sun, pushed by the solar wind.
The asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter is mostly empty space. Spacecraft fly through it without hitting anything.
There may be more planets than stars in the Milky Way.
Astronomers have confirmed more than 5,000 planets orbiting other stars.
The universe is about 13.8 billion years old.
The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across.
Most of the universe is made of dark energy and dark matter, which we can't see directly.
Ordinary matter, everything we can see and touch, makes up only about 5% of the universe.
