# Space — batch 5: weird & wonderful space facts
There's a giant cloud of alcohol near the centre of the Milky Way, in a region called Sagittarius B2.
Some molecules in Sagittarius B2 give it a raspberry-like flavour compound, ethyl formate.
Scientists have found water vapour around a quasar billions of light-years away, holding far more water than all of Earth's oceans.
There's a planet, TrES-2b, that reflects less than 1% of the light that hits it, making it darker than coal.
On the exoplanet HD 189733b, scientists think it may rain glass sideways in howling winds.
Some exoplanets may have oceans of lava.
On Neptune and Uranus, extreme pressure may squeeze carbon into diamonds that "rain" down.
Mars has auroras too. In 2024, a rover captured one that would be visible to human eyes.
Jupiter has the brightest auroras in the solar system.
Jupiter gives off more heat than it receives from the Sun.
Saturn's rings are relatively young, perhaps only a few hundred million years old, according to some studies.
Saturn's rings are slowly disappearing, raining down onto the planet.
One of Saturn's moons, Pan, is shaped like a ravioli or a walnut.
Saturn's moon Hyperion looks like a giant sponge.
Saturn's moon Phoebe orbits backwards and may be a captured object from the outer solar system.
Uranus smells like rotten eggs, scientists think, because of hydrogen sulfide in its clouds.
Mercury is shrinking slowly as its core cools.
Mercury has a tail, like a comet, made of sodium.
Venus has no moons.
Earth has had "mini-moons": small asteroids temporarily captured in orbit.
Earth isn't round in a perfect way. It's slightly pear-shaped.
The Moon's surface temperature swings from about 120 °C in sunlight to about −130 °C in darkness.
There's no "dark side of the Moon," just a far side that we never see from Earth.
A day on the Moon, from sunrise to sunrise, lasts about 29.5 Earth days.
The Moon is shrinking a little too, wrinkling its surface as it cools.
Scientists found that some lunar soil can grow plants in lab experiments.
Moon rocks brought back by Apollo are still studied today.
China's Chang'e 5 brought back Moon samples in 2020.
Chang'e 6 brought back the first samples from the far side of the Moon in 2024.
There's a mountain on the asteroid Vesta, Rheasilvia's central peak, about 20 km tall.
The asteroid Psyche may be the exposed metal core of an ancient planet-like body. NASA launched a mission there in 2023.
The asteroid belt's total mass is only about 3% of the Moon's mass.
Some asteroids have their own moons.
The asteroid Dinkinesh has a tiny moon that turned out to be two rocks stuck together.
Comet 67P, studied by Rosetta, smelled like rotten eggs, horse stables and bitter almonds, based on its chemistry.
Interstellar objects, like 'Oumuamua in 2017, have passed through our solar system from other star systems.
'Oumuamua was shaped like a long cigar or a pancake. Scientists still debate its nature.
A second interstellar visitor, comet 2I/Borisov, was found in 2019.
Some stars are so cool you could touch them without being burned, in theory. Some brown dwarfs are about room temperature.
WISE 0855 is a brown dwarf with a temperature below freezing, the coldest known object of its kind.
A star called Methuselah, HD 140283, seems almost as old as the universe itself.
The fastest-spinning known star rotates hundreds of times per second.
The largest known structure in the universe may be a vast wall of galaxies billions of light-years across.
The Boötes Void is an enormous region of space with very few galaxies.
Some galaxies have no detectable dark matter, which puzzles astronomers.
Some galaxies seem to be almost entirely dark matter.
The Milky Way is slowly eating smaller galaxies, like the Sagittarius Dwarf Galaxy.
Our galaxy has a warp, like the brim of a hat.
Scientists have found giant bubbles of gas, called the Fermi bubbles, above and below the Milky Way's centre.
The Sun's magnetic field flips every 11 years or so.
The Sun is losing mass constantly, through light and the solar wind.
The Sun's light takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth, but its gravity's effects also travel at the speed of light.
Earth is passing through debris from comets several times a year, causing meteor showers.
About 100 tonnes of space dust may fall on Earth every day, according to older estimates. Newer estimates suggest less.
Micrometeorites can be found on city rooftops.
Every year, a few small meteorites land in India too, and some are collected for research.
If Jupiter had been about 80 times more massive, it could have become a star.
Neptune radiates more than twice as much energy as it gets from the Sun.
The Great Dark Spot on Neptune, seen by Voyager 2, later vanished.
A huge storm on Saturn in 2010–11 wrapped all the way around the planet.
Dust devils on Mars have helped clean rover solar panels, extending missions.
The Opportunity rover was planned for 90 days but lasted almost 15 years.
Opportunity's last message was interpreted as "My battery is low and it's getting dark."
NASA's Spirit rover got stuck in sand in 2009 and was declared lost in 2011.
The Curiosity rover played "Happy Birthday" to itself on Mars in 2013, its first anniversary there.
The Perseverance rover carries a plaque honouring healthcare workers from the COVID-19 pandemic.
Perseverance has collected rock samples that future missions may bring back to Earth.
Some Martian meteorites contain tiny structures once claimed to be fossils of microbes. Most scientists doubt it.
Venus may once have had oceans before a runaway greenhouse effect boiled them away.
Titan's lakes are so still that their surfaces act like mirrors.
Ganymede may have an underground ocean too.
Enceladus's plumes contain salts, organic molecules and phosphorus, some of the ingredients for life.
Pluto may have a hidden ocean beneath its icy shell.
The dwarf planet Haumea is shaped like an egg and spins every four hours.
Haumea has rings, discovered in 2017.
Sedna, a distant dwarf planet candidate, takes over 10,000 years to orbit the Sun.
Some astronomers think a hidden "Planet Nine" may lurk beyond Neptune, but it hasn't been found.
