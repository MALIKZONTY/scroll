# Space — batch 2: planets & moons
Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system, only slightly larger than our Moon.
Mercury has ice inside permanently shadowed craters near its poles, despite being closest to the Sun.
Venus is the brightest planet in our night sky, often called the morning or evening star.
Venus's clouds are made largely of sulfuric acid.
The air pressure on Venus's surface is about 90 times that on Earth, like being 900 metres underwater.
Soviet Venera landers sent back the first pictures from Venus's surface in the 1970s, before being crushed and cooked.
Earth is the only planet known to have plate tectonics today.
Mars is about half the size of Earth.
Gravity on Mars is about 38% of Earth's. You could jump nearly three times higher there.
A day on Mars, called a sol, is about 24 hours and 39 minutes long.
A year on Mars lasts about 687 Earth days.
Mars has the biggest dust storms in the solar system. Some cover the entire planet.
Mars has polar ice caps made of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide.
NASA's Perseverance rover landed on Mars in 2021, carrying a small helicopter called Ingenuity.
Ingenuity made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet in April 2021.
The Curiosity rover has been exploring Mars since 2012.
Mars once had rivers and lakes. Rovers have found ancient riverbeds and deltas.
Phobos, Mars's larger moon, is slowly spiralling inwards and may break apart or crash in tens of millions of years.
Jupiter is more than 300 times the mass of Earth.
Jupiter has the strongest magnetic field of any planet.
Jupiter's moon Io is the most volcanically active place in the solar system.
Io's volcanoes are powered by tidal squeezing from Jupiter's gravity.
Jupiter's moon Callisto is one of the most heavily cratered objects in the solar system.
Ganymede is the only moon known to have its own magnetic field.
Jupiter acts like a cosmic vacuum cleaner, pulling in or flinging away many comets and asteroids.
In 1994, pieces of comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 crashed into Jupiter, leaving dark scars bigger than Earth.
Saturn has more known moons than any other planet, well over a hundred.
Saturn's hexagon is a six-sided jet stream pattern at its north pole, wider than Earth.
Saturn is so far away that sunlight there is about 1% as bright as on Earth.
NASA's Cassini spacecraft studied Saturn for 13 years before diving into the planet in 2017.
The Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, the most distant landing ever made.
Titan is the only moon with a thick atmosphere.
Titan's atmosphere is so thick and its gravity so low that a human could fly there with wings strapped to their arms.
Saturn's moon Iapetus has one bright side and one dark side, like a yin-yang.
Saturn's moon Mimas has a giant crater that makes it look like the Death Star.
Uranus was the first planet discovered with a telescope, by William Herschel in 1781.
Uranus is the coldest planetary atmosphere in the solar system, dropping to about −224 °C.
Uranus has faint rings, discovered in 1977.
Uranus's moons are named after characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope.
Neptune takes about 165 Earth years to orbit the Sun once.
Since its discovery in 1846, Neptune completed its first full orbit only in 2011.
Neptune's largest moon, Triton, orbits backwards compared with the planet's spin.
Triton has geysers that shoot nitrogen gas into its thin atmosphere.
Triton is slowly spiralling towards Neptune and may one day be torn apart into rings.
Only one spacecraft, Voyager 2, has ever visited Uranus and Neptune.
Pluto is smaller than Earth's Moon.
Pluto's largest moon, Charon, is so big that the two orbit a point in space between them.
NASA's New Horizons flew past Pluto in 2015, giving us the first close-up pictures.
Pluto has mountains of water ice as tall as the Rockies.
A year on Pluto lasts about 248 Earth years.
Since its discovery in 1930, Pluto hasn't yet completed a full orbit of the Sun.
Ceres, in the asteroid belt, is a dwarf planet too.
Eris, a dwarf planet beyond Pluto, is about the same size as Pluto but more massive.
The Moon is about a quarter of Earth's width.
The Moon's gravity is about one-sixth of Earth's.
The Moon likely formed from debris after a Mars-sized object smashed into the young Earth.
The Moon has no real atmosphere, so its sky is black even during the day.
The dark patches on the Moon are ancient lava plains called maria, meaning "seas."
Moon dust is sharp and clingy. Apollo astronauts said it smelled like spent gunpowder.
The Moon has water ice in permanently shadowed craters near its poles.
The far side of the Moon isn't dark. It gets just as much sunlight as the near side.
China's Chang'e 4 made the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon in 2019.
A solar eclipse happens because the Moon is about 400 times smaller than the Sun but also about 400 times closer.
Total solar eclipses won't last forever. As the Moon drifts away, it will one day be too small to cover the Sun completely.
During a total lunar eclipse, the Moon turns reddish. It's sometimes called a blood moon.
Earth's shadow on the Moon during an eclipse is curved, one early clue that Earth is round.
The Moon is the only place beyond Earth where humans have set foot.
The last people to walk on the Moon were the crew of Apollo 17, in December 1972.
Apollo astronauts left mirrors on the Moon. Scientists still bounce lasers off them to measure the distance.
There are about 96 bags of human waste left on the Moon from the Apollo missions.
A golf ball hit on the Moon by Alan Shepard in 1971 is still up there.
Ganymede, Titan, Callisto and Io are all bigger than our Moon. Ganymede and Titan are bigger than Mercury.
Europa's ice shell may be 15 to 25 km thick, with an ocean beneath that could hold more water than all of Earth's oceans.
Enceladus is one of the most reflective objects in the solar system because of its fresh ice.
The planets all orbit the Sun in roughly the same flat plane, a leftover from the disc they formed in.
Venus and Uranus are the only planets that spin in the opposite direction to most.
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune have no solid surface to stand on.
Uranus and Neptune are called ice giants because they contain lots of water, ammonia and methane ices.
Neptune's blue colour comes partly from methane in its atmosphere.
The Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune is a huge ring of icy objects, including Pluto.
The Oort Cloud, a giant shell of icy bodies, may surround the solar system far beyond Pluto.
Many long-period comets are thought to come from the Oort Cloud.
