# Space — batch 3: missions, astronauts & life in orbit
Astronauts on the ISS see the Sun rise or set roughly every 45 minutes.
The ISS travels at about 28,000 km/h.
The ISS is about the size of a football field, including its solar panels.
The ISS has been continuously occupied by humans since November 2000.
You can sometimes spot the ISS from Earth with your bare eyes. It looks like a bright, fast-moving star.
Astronauts on the ISS drink water recycled from sweat, breath moisture and urine.
In space, tears don't fall. They stick to your eyes in a ball.
Astronauts can't burp properly in space, because gas and liquid don't separate in the stomach.
Without gravity, fluids shift towards the head, making astronauts' faces look puffy.
Astronauts lose bone and muscle mass in space, so they exercise about two hours a day.
Astronauts sleep in sleeping bags attached to the wall so they don't float around.
In space, your sense of taste can dull, so many astronauts love spicy food and hot sauce.
Food crumbs are dangerous in space because they can float into equipment or eyes. That's why tortillas are popular instead of bread.
Astronauts' hair and nails keep growing in space, so they cut them near a vacuum hose.
A spacesuit can take about 45 minutes or more to put on with help.
NASA's spacesuits for spacewalks have cost millions of dollars each.
The first spacewalk was made by Soviet cosmonaut Alexei Leonov in 1965.
Leonov's suit ballooned in the vacuum of space, and he had to let air out to squeeze back inside.
Apollo 13's oxygen tank exploded in 1970, but the crew made it home safely.
"Houston, we've had a problem" is what the Apollo 13 crew actually said.
The Apollo 11 computer was far less powerful than a modern calculator.
The Saturn V rocket that took astronauts to the Moon is still one of the most powerful rockets ever launched.
Michael Collins orbited the Moon alone while Armstrong and Aldrin walked on it.
Buzz Aldrin used a felt-tip pen to fix a broken switch so Apollo 11 could take off from the Moon.
The US flags planted on the Moon have probably been bleached white by sunlight.
Sputnik 1, launched in 1957, was the first artificial satellite. It was about the size of a beach ball.
There are thousands of active satellites orbiting Earth today.
Space junk, from old satellites and rocket parts, is a growing problem in Earth's orbit.
Even a tiny paint fleck in orbit can hit hard enough to chip a spacecraft window.
India's ISRO launched 104 satellites on a single rocket in 2017, a record at the time.
Mangalyaan, India's Mars mission, cost less than many Hollywood space movies.
ISRO's first rocket parts were famously carried by bicycle in the 1960s.
Kalpana Chawla was the first woman of Indian origin to go to space, in 1997.
Sunita Williams, an astronaut of Indian descent, has spent hundreds of days in space.
Aditya-L1 is India's first mission to study the Sun.
Shubhanshu Shukla became the first Indian astronaut to visit the ISS, in 2025.
India's Gaganyaan programme aims to launch Indian astronauts on an Indian rocket.
Chandrayaan-1 helped confirm water molecules on the Moon in 2009.
Chandrayaan-3's landing site is named Shiv Shakti Point.
NASA's Parker Solar Probe is the fastest human-made object ever, flying close to the Sun.
The Parker Solar Probe has flown through the Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona.
The Voyager probes were launched in 1977 to take advantage of a rare planetary alignment.
Voyager 1 took the "Pale Blue Dot" photo in 1990, showing Earth as a tiny speck.
The New Horizons probe carries some of the ashes of Clyde Tombaugh, who discovered Pluto.
The Rosetta mission landed a probe, Philae, on a comet in 2014.
Japan's Hayabusa2 brought back samples from the asteroid Ryugu.
NASA's OSIRIS-REx collected samples from the asteroid Bennu and returned them to Earth in 2023.
NASA's DART mission crashed into an asteroid in 2022 and changed its orbit, testing planetary defence.
The Hubble telescope was launched with a flawed mirror. Astronauts fixed it in space in 1993.
The James Webb Space Telescope's mirror is made of 18 gold-coated hexagonal segments.
Webb's sunshield is about the size of a tennis court.
Webb sees in infrared, letting it peer through dust and look back to the early universe.
The Spitzer and Kepler telescopes helped discover thousands of planets around other stars.
Kepler found that small planets around other stars are common.
SpaceX became the first private company to send astronauts to the ISS, in 2020.
Reusable rockets that land themselves upright are now routine.
The first tourist to visit space paid for a trip to the ISS in 2001.
A Tesla car and a mannequin called "Starman" were launched into orbit around the Sun in 2018.
Astronauts brought back about 382 kg of Moon rock and soil during the Apollo missions.
Some Moon rocks are older than almost any rocks found on Earth.
Astronauts in orbit aren't weightless because there's no gravity. They're constantly falling around Earth.
At the ISS's height, gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground.
Astronauts use special toilets with suction instead of gravity.
The first meal eaten on the Moon was by Apollo 11's crew, and it included bacon squares.
Laika's flight helped prove that living things could survive launch and orbit.
Ham the chimpanzee flew into space in 1961, before the first American astronaut.
Fruit flies were the first animals launched to space, in 1947.
Tardigrades have survived exposure to the vacuum of space in experiments.
Plants have been grown on the ISS, including lettuce that astronauts ate.
Valentina Tereshkova orbited Earth 48 times on her solo mission in 1963.
Mae Jemison became the first African American woman in space in 1992.
The longest single spaceflight by a human lasted about 437 days, by cosmonaut Valeri Polyakov.
More than 600 people have travelled to space.
Some astronauts report a "space smell" after spacewalks, often described as like burnt steak or metal.
Many astronauts describe the "overview effect": a deep sense of awe seeing Earth from space.
From space, astronauts can see city lights, lightning storms and auroras at night.
The Moon landings left behind seismometers, reflectors and lots of equipment that's still there.
Some astronauts have had their wedding rings, family photos and personal items flown to space.
Space agencies are planning to send humans back to the Moon in the coming years through NASA's Artemis programme.
