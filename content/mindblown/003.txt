# Mind-blowing — batch 3: the human body
Your body has about 37 trillion cells, and most of them are red blood cells.
Your thigh bone, the femur, is the longest and strongest bone in your body.
The smallest bone in your body, the stapes, is in your ear and is about the size of a grain of rice.
Your skin is your body's largest organ.
You shed tens of thousands of skin cells every minute.
Much of household dust includes dead human skin cells.
Your heart pumps about 7,000 litres of blood a day.
Your blood travels through about 100,000 km of blood vessels.
A red blood cell can travel around your entire body in about a minute.
Your body makes millions of new red blood cells every second.
Your small intestine is about 6 to 7 metres long.
Your stomach acid is strong enough to dissolve some metals.
You produce about 1 to 1.5 litres of saliva a day.
Your nose and sinuses produce a lot of mucus every day, and most of it you swallow without noticing.
The average person blinks about 15 to 20 times a minute.
Your eyes can distinguish millions of different colours.
Your eye's lens focuses images upside down. Your brain flips them.
The cornea is one of the only parts of the body with no blood supply. It gets oxygen from the air.
Your eye muscles are among the most active muscles in your body.
Your ears keep growing slowly throughout your life.
Your sense of smell is linked directly to the part of the brain that handles memory and emotion.
Humans can detect some smells at incredibly tiny concentrations.
You can't tickle yourself, because your brain predicts your own touch.
The brain has around 86 billion neurons.
Nerve signals can travel at over 400 km/h.
The brain is about 60% fat.
Your brain is more active while you sleep than you might think, especially during dreaming.
Most people dream several times a night, even if they don't remember.
You can't breathe and swallow at the exact same time.
Humans are among the best long-distance runners in the animal kingdom, thanks to sweating.
You have around 2 to 4 million sweat glands.
Babies have more taste buds than adults, including some on the roof of the mouth and cheeks.
Your tongue print is unique, like a fingerprint.
Teeth are the only part of your body that can't repair themselves.
Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in the human body.
Your fingernails grow about 3 mm a month.
Hair grows about 1 cm a month.
An average person has about 100,000 hairs on their head.
Hair is so strong that a single strand can hold around 100 grams.
Humans and giraffes have the same number of vertebrae in their necks.
Your body has more bacterial genes than human genes.
Your gut bacteria may influence your mood and appetite.
The appendix may store good gut bacteria, according to some scientists.
Your liver can regrow even after a large part of it is removed.
Your kidneys filter about 180 litres of blood plasma a day.
Your lungs, spread out flat, would cover roughly half a tennis court, by some estimates.
The left lung is smaller than the right, to make room for the heart.
You breathe more than 20,000 times a day.
Each time you sneeze, air can rush out of your nose at high speed.
Your stomach "growls" whether you're hungry or not. It's just louder when it's empty.
Goosebumps are a leftover reflex from our hairy ancestors, who puffed up their fur to stay warm or look bigger.
Your body has a natural clock that controls sleep, hunger and alertness.
Your body temperature is slightly lower in the early morning.
Humans are the only animals that cry emotional tears, as far as scientists know.
Your body contains enough iron to make a small nail.
Your body contains about 0.2 milligrams of gold, mostly in your blood.
About 60% of an adult's body is water.
Your bones are constantly being rebuilt. You get a new skeleton roughly every 10 years.
Muscles can only pull, never push.
Your heart has its own electrical system and can keep beating briefly outside the body.
Your body makes roughly 3.8 million new cells every second, according to a 2021 estimate.
Fingerprints form before birth and stay the same for life.
Identical twins don't have identical fingerprints.
Babies are born without kneecaps made of bone. They're cartilage at first.
A newborn baby can see only about 20 to 30 cm clearly, just about the distance to a parent's face.
Your sense of balance comes from fluid in your inner ear.
Spinning makes you dizzy because the fluid in your ears keeps moving after you stop.
Humans have unique scent signatures. Dogs can tell people apart by smell.
Laughing increases your heart rate and can burn a few extra calories.
Your body has more than 600 muscles.
The muscles that focus your eyes move about 100,000 times a day.
Your body contains about 5 litres of blood.
