# Mind-blowing — batch 5: food, everyday objects & surprising origins
Honey can last for thousands of years without spoiling, if it's sealed.
Cashews grow attached to the bottom of a fruit called the cashew apple.
Pineapples take about two years to grow.
Pineapples contain an enzyme that breaks down proteins, which is why your mouth tingles after eating them.
Peanuts grow underground, not on trees.
Almonds are seeds, related to peaches and plums.
Strawberries aren't berries, but bananas, grapes and tomatoes are.
Avocados are berries too.
Cucumbers and pumpkins are botanically fruits.
Rhubarb is a vegetable, though it's often eaten like a fruit.
Potatoes were first domesticated in the Andes of South America.
Chillies came to India from the Americas, brought by Portuguese traders.
Before chillies arrived, Indian food used black pepper and long pepper for heat.
Tomatoes and potatoes also arrived in India from the Americas.
Tea became widely popular in India only in the 20th century, partly thanks to advertising campaigns.
Samosas likely came to India from Central Asia and the Middle East.
Gulab jamun is thought to have roots in a Persian dessert.
Biryani has Persian influences. The name may come from the Persian word "birian," meaning fried.
Jalebi has relatives across the Middle East, like zalabiya.
Instant noodles were invented in Japan in 1958.
Ice cream cones became popular at the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis.
Popsicles were invented by an 11-year-old boy who left a cup of soda outside overnight.
Chocolate chip cookies were invented in the 1930s at the Toll House Inn in the USA.
Sandwiches are named after the Earl of Sandwich, who reportedly ate meat between bread so he could keep playing cards.
The croissant became iconic in France, but its ancestor, the kipferl, came from Austria.
Fortune cookies are thought to have been popularised in the USA, likely with Japanese roots, not Chinese.
Caesar salad was invented in Mexico, by an Italian immigrant named Caesar Cardini.
French fries probably come from Belgium, or perhaps France. People still argue.
Wasabi in most restaurants is actually horseradish coloured green.
White chocolate isn't technically chocolate, because it contains no cocoa solids.
Apples, pears and roses are in the same plant family.
Ginger, turmeric and cardamom all belong to the same plant family.
Cinnamon comes from the inner bark of a tree.
Saffron comes from the stigmas of a crocus flower and is picked by hand.
Kashmir is one of the few places in the world where saffron is grown.
Vanilla is the second most expensive spice after saffron.
Cardamom is sometimes called the "queen of spices," and black pepper the "king of spices."
Nutmeg and mace come from the same fruit.
Sugar was first refined from sugarcane in ancient India.
The word "sugar" comes from the Sanskrit "sharkara."
"Candy" may come from the Sanskrit "khanda," meaning a piece of sugar.
Coconut water can be used as an emergency IV fluid in rare cases, and was during World War II.
Ghee can last for months without refrigeration.
Rice is the staple food for more than half the world's population.
India is one of the world's biggest producers of milk.
India grows a large share of the world's bananas and mangoes.
There are more than 1,000 varieties of mango.
Alphonso mangoes are named after Afonso de Albuquerque, a Portuguese general.
The mango is the national fruit of India.
Ripe cranberries bounce.
Carrots can't actually give you night vision. That story was spread by the British in World War II to hide their radar technology.
Eating celery doesn't use more calories than it contains. That's a myth.
The holes in Swiss cheese are called "eyes."
Chewing gum doesn't stay in your stomach for seven years.
The red colouring in some sweets and yoghurts comes from crushed insects called cochineal.
Jelly beans' shiny coating can come from shellac, made by lac insects.
Lac insects are farmed in India, which is one of the world's biggest producers of lac.
The "use by" and "best before" dates mean different things. "Best before" is about quality, not safety.
The first toothbrush with bristles was made in China, using hog hairs.
Before toilet paper, people used leaves, moss, corncobs and even shells.
Soap dates back at least 4,500 years, to ancient Mesopotamia.
The zip was invented in the early 1900s and was first called a "clasp locker."
Umbrellas were first used for shade, not rain.
Sunglasses were first used in China, made from smoky quartz, possibly to hide judges' expressions.
Glass is made mostly from sand.
Pencils have never contained lead. They contain graphite.
The eraser on a pencil was added in the 1850s.
The paperclip as we know it today became common in the 1890s.
Paper money was first used in China, more than 1,000 years ago.
The first coins were made in Lydia, in modern-day Turkey, around 600 BCE.
The first credit card, the Diners Club card, was introduced in 1950.
India's UPI handles billions of transactions every month, one of the largest real-time payment systems in the world.
The word "jeans" comes from Genoa, in Italy, where the fabric was made.
Denim comes from "serge de Nîmes," a fabric from Nîmes in France.
Dungarees get their name from Dongri, an area of Mumbai, where the fabric was made.
Pyjamas come from the Hindi-Urdu "pai jama," meaning leg garment.
Chintz, the patterned cloth, comes from the Hindi word "chheent."
